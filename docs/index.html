How to read it

  • Neighboring zones touch to make the regional sequence legible; gaps retain major deserts and high mountains. Their hand-drawn boundaries are approximate, not surveyed limits or proof of continuous settlement.
  • Muted yellow date outlines and pale color identify interpolated dates, uncertain geographical extents, or evidence for grain presence, herding or contact rather than established local crop cultivation. Arrows are schematic connections; inferred routes remain hypotheses.
  • Older accounts date Mehrgarh to about 7000 BCE. The 2025 study dates its cemetery, so earlier occupation at the site is not fully ruled out. Either way, its wheat and barley came from the west.
  • Arrows show crops and herding moving, not necessarily people. Indus Valley genomes have Iranian-related ancestry that split from the Zagros farmer line before farming began (Shinde et al. 2019).
  • East of the Indus, these crops joined local farming: rice on the Ganges, millets and pulses in the south, millet in China.
  • The Levant and central Anatolia appear faintly to orient the western branch, whose expansion belongs to the western map.
