One date scale

Rust and ochre mark earlier cultivation; yellow, green, teal and blue mark later regional milestones. All branches use the same chronology scale. A date describes the named regional crop milestone, rather than the appearance of every crop grown there.

Many agricultural histories

Wheat and barley, rice, millets, American seed crops and tubers, African cereals, and New Guinean and Pacific gardening retain their separate histories. Similar dates do not imply a shared origin or a single migrating population.

Approximate boundaries

The colored extents are hand-drawn reconstructions. Pale areas and question marks identify estimated extents or chronology. Arrows are proposed crop connections. Early cultivation, domestication, established farming and island settlement are different forms of evidence; the ledger qualifies them.

Regional dates and evidence

Rounded BCE and CE dates. Expand a region to inspect the underlying milestone and source; some small or crowded map labels appear only here. The oldest cultivation evidence does not necessarily date fully domesticated crops or farming across the entire polygon.

Sources and chronology notes
  1. Gronenborn, Horejs, Hoffmann, Börner and Ober 2026.2, Expansion of farming in western Eurasia · CC BY 4.0 — Primary source for the user-supplied western reference. The authors describe a condensed and simplified map; dates are approximate calibrated BCE. The new polygons are schematic adaptations, not the original georeferenced data or measured boundaries.
  2. Bishop et al. 2022, Scotland's first farmers: new insights into early farming practices in north-west Europe — Archaeobotanical cereal evidence from early fourth-millennium Scotland, including Dubton Farm phase 1 at c. 3940–3659 cal BCE. A 3800 BCE regional display label is rounded; broad Scottish coverage is approximate.
  3. Dulias et al. 2022, Ancient DNA at the edge of the world — Archaeological background identifies a flourishing farming economy in Neolithic Orkney, 3800–2500 BCE. The 3700 BCE island label summarizes early crop farming, not a directly dated frontier.
  4. Hjelle et al. 2017, Prehistoric agriculture in western Norway: botanical evidence — Separates early landscape disturbance from cultivation. Cereal macroremains and arable weeds establish crop farming by the Late Neolithic, 2300–1800 BCE; the conservative 2300 BCE label does not date earlier pastoral activity as cultivation.
  5. Malleson 2026, Cereals, UCLA Encyclopedia of Egyptology — Specialist review of Egyptian crop evidence places earliest Fayum cereals near 5000 BCE with broad dating uncertainty. The Nile/floodplain extents and a later upper-valley 4000 BCE label are regional syntheses, not a claim that Egypt was uniformly cultivated.
  6. Eastern farming map: regional chronology and linked evidence — Reuses the reviewed regional dates, with secondary-source dates retained as approximate. Earlier pastoralism, Ubaid contact, and traded-grain-only zones are omitted from this crop map.
  7. Motuzaite Matuzeviciute et al. 2022: Central Tian Shan farming — Chap II occupation modeled to 2474–2162 cal BCE; cereal chaff supports local cultivation and processing.
  8. Mutin et al. 2025: Mehrgarh chronology — Cemetery start 5223–4914 BCE; this does not exclude earlier occupation elsewhere at the site.
  9. Riehl, Zeidi and Conard 2013: cultivation at Chogha Golan — Long-term plant management precedes morphologically domesticated emmer. The early map date includes cultivation, not only fully domesticated cereals.
  10. Hili 8: early oasis agriculture — Excavation report dates the earliest building to about 3000 BCE and reports cereals. Coverage is restricted to the Hajar foothill oases.
  11. Agricultural terraces in Yemen — Cultivation in the highlands is represented conservatively around 2500 BCE; this does not extend earlier inland cattle evidence to the coast.
  12. Spengler Lab: Kazakhstan archaeobotanical fieldwork — Late Bronze Age cultivation and processing replace the earlier Begash funerary-grain date as the basis for colored farming coverage.
  13. Gao et al. 2021: Qugong crop assemblage — Crop assemblage around 3400 cal BP, approximately 1450 BCE; a 2024 site model instead places the early phase around 1400–1300 BCE.
  14. Smith 2001 — Documenting plant domestication — Guilá Naquitz domesticated squash around 10,000 calibrated years BP; maize macrofossils about 6,300 cal BP. Mexican crop histories differ by species. Broad regional connections on this map are cartographic estimates, not newly dated boundaries.
  15. Ranere et al. 2009 — Early maize and squash in the Central Balsas — Xihuatoxtla crop evidence in contexts around 8,700 cal BP, approximately 6750 BCE. The small patch denotes local early cultivation, not all Mesoamerica.
  16. Merrill et al. 2009 — The diffusion of maize to the southwestern United States — Maize is established in the US Southwest by 2100 cal BCE. The northern Mexican connector and later northern extent are schematic; adoption was uneven and crop presence did not immediately imply staple dependence.
  17. Smith and Yarnell 2009 — An indigenous crop complex in eastern North America — Phillips Spring squash: 5025 cal BP (about 3100 BCE); Hayes sunflower: 4840 cal BP (about 2900 BCE); Riverton crop complex: 3800 cal BP (1850 BCE). These are independent indigenous crop histories. Regional coverage remains schematic.
  18. Katzenberg et al. 1995 — Maize horticulture and paleodiet in southern Ontario — Isotopic evidence shows increasing maize contribution between 650 and 1250 CE. The 800 and 1000 CE northern zones summarize regional adoption within that broad interval, not exact first-arrival boundaries.
  19. Kennett et al. 2020 — Early isotopic evidence for maize as a staple grain in the Americas — Belize dietary transition at 4700–4000 cal BP, approximately 2750–2050 BCE. The rounded 2500 BCE zone illustrates increasing maize reliance; some crops and maize presence were earlier.
  20. Dickau, Ranere and Cooke 2007 — Preceramic dispersals of maize and root crops in Panama — Central Panama maize at 7800–7000 cal BP; maize and root crops in western Panama at 7400–5600 cal BP. A small early patch is shown around 5800 BCE; routes between sampled areas are inferred.
  21. Grobman et al. 2012 — Preceramic maize from Paredones and Huaca Prieta, Peru — Northern Peruvian maize macrofossils directly dated to 6775–6504 cal BP, approximately 4825–4554 BCE. Maize joined older local horticultural systems; this is not the first cultivation of every crop in Peru.
  22. Rumold and Aldenderfer 2016 — Potato evidence at Jiskairumoko — Cultivated or domesticated potato starch occurs in Late Archaic–Early Formative contexts. The local 3000 BCE patch is rounded within the regional Terminal Archaic chronology; it is not a precise domestication year.
  23. Bruno 2023 — Archaeobotanical insights into kañawa domestication — The southern Titicaca record documents established quinoa and tuber farming before later kañawa cultivation. The broad 1500 BCE Andean band is schematic regional coverage around an established Formative farming horizon.
  24. Lombardo et al. 2020 — Early Holocene crop cultivation in southwest Amazonia — Managed forest islands in Llanos de Moxos contain Manihot around 10,350 cal BP and squash around 10,250 cal BP. The 8400 BCE patch is deliberately local; it does not paint the whole Amazon as early farmland.
  25. Watling et al. 2018 — Southwestern Amazonia as an early food-production centre — Teotônio manioc and squash cultivation is associated with contexts around 6500–5500 cal BP, roughly 4550–3550 BCE. Later river-corridor coverage shown here is inferred and does not date an entire forest boundary.
  26. Pagán-Jiménez et al. 2015 — Early dispersals of food plants into the southern Caribbean — Southern Caribbean and northeastern South American cultigen use reaches at least 7790 cal BP. This early use is distinguished from the later expansion and intensification of island horticulture.
  27. Mickleburgh and Pagán-Jiménez 2012 — Food plants in pre-Columbian Caribbean dental calculus — Plant management predates the Ceramic Age; horticulture intensified from roughly 400 BCE onward. The 400 BCE zone is a later regional horticultural horizon, not the first appearance of crops on every island.
  28. Planella et al. 2019 — Quinoa in pre-Hispanic central Chile — Late Archaic central Chile includes quinoa remains; the Las Morrenas record is dated around 3250–2980 cal BP (1300–1030 BCE). A narrow 1200 BCE zone summarizes this local crop history.
  29. Campbell et al. 2020 — Diet in Southern Chile, 36°–42°S — Regional isotopic synthesis discusses variable crop use at the southern limit of precontact agriculture. The 1000 CE patch is an approximate later farming extent, not a uniform first-adoption date.
  30. Lu et al. 2009, Earliest domestication of common millet — Cishan phytolith/storage evidence spans 10,300–8,700 cal BP. A conservative 6500 BCE display label is used for a crop-producing North China region; this is not a single precise domestication date.

  32. Fuller 2011, Pathways to Asian civilizations — Specialist synthesis of distinct Asian rice histories and dispersal. Regional polygons and connecting dates remain schematic; local South Asian cultivation is not a direct extension of Chinese rice farming.
