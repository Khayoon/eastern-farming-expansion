Reading the date colors

Colors run from dark red-brown through rust, orange and tan, then blues, greens and finally bright yellow for the youngest date. Each map uses its full date range: 10,000 BCE–1500 CE in the world view and 9,600 BCE – 1,330 CE in the combined Eurasia view. A muted yellow outline on a number marks an inferred or uncertain date or geographic extent; the source notes explain which. “By” dates mark documented cultivation, not a known first arrival.

Many agricultural histories

Wheat and barley, rice, millets, American seed crops and tubers, African cereals, and New Guinean and Pacific gardening retain their separate histories. Similar dates do not imply a shared origin or a single migrating population.

Land and local evidence

Broad regional outlines generalize cultivation across plains and foothills, with major desert and mountain gaps. Individually evidenced oases retain local footprints. South American and introduced central/southern African regions retain the shared regional reconstruction. Color indicates cultivation within a landscape, including uncultivated land. Uncertain connections, deserts and high-plateau interiors can remain unfilled. The ledger distinguishes local evidence, inferred extent and proposed routes.

Regional dates and evidence

Rounded BCE and CE dates. Expand a region to inspect the underlying milestone and source; some small or crowded map labels appear only here. The oldest cultivation evidence does not necessarily date fully domesticated crops or farming across the entire polygon.
